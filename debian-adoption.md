## 1. Distributions that moved to Debian

Some Linux distributions that previously relied on Ubuntu are moving directly to Debian, while others have been Debian-based for years.

The reasons tend to be similar:

- Greater independence from Canonical.
- Less dependence on Snap.
- More control over release cycles.
- Direct access to Debian repositories and packaging.
- Greater alignment with the Debian ecosystem.
  
As of November 2026, these are the projects worth looking at.

- Tuxedo OS   (Switched to Debian in 2026)
- Vanilla OS  (Switched to Debian in 2023)
- Peppermint  (Switched to Debian in 2022)
- Nitrux      (Switched to Debian in 2021)
- gLinux      (Google's internal distro, switched to Debian in 2018)
- Deepin      (Switched to Debian in 2015, independent since deepin 23)
- Kali Linux  (Switched to Debian in 2013, replacing Ubuntu-based BackTrack)
- Pardus      (Switched to Debian in 2013)
